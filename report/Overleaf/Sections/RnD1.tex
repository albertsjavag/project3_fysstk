\subsection{The scheme}

First, to gain some visual understanding of the problem, we plot the 3D-surface of the closed form solution of the call option. The edges $T - t= 0$ and $T -t = 1$ is marked in red, clearly showing how the solution evolves from the initial kink at $S = K$ to a smooth solution. As discussed in Sec~\ref{sec:theory}, we change variable to  $x = \log(S/K)$ so strike lies at $x = 0$. Our domain is $x \in [-1, 1]$ which corresponds to $S \in [36.8, 271.8]$.

We study the numerical solution of the option with the explicit scheme as discussed in Sec~\ref{sec:theory}. Figure~\ref{fig:fd_profiles} show the absolute error between the scheme and the exact solution for partitions $\Delta x = 0.1$ and $=0.01$, focusing 1 week to maturity and 1 year. Both $\Delta x = 0.1$ and $0.01$ have a fixed $\alpha = \Delta \tau \,/ \, \Delta x^2 \approx 0.0385$ which is justified in Sec~\ref{sec:implementation}. 

Before beginning, we run the scheme for $\sin(\pi x)$, and found that the error fall exactly like $\Delta x^2$ with a slope of $2$. Hence, making the grid $10$ times finer results in $100$ times less error. We also found that the absolute error is greater for $\tau = 0.2$ than for $\tau = 0.02$. 

After a successful verification, we can apply the same scheme to the Option Pricing framework. Our main findings are that the error lies at $x = 0$ (i.e. in the kink where $S=K$) in 3 out of the 4 runs, and the exception is for 1 year ($1/100$) where the max error lies at $ x = 0.01$ , right beside zero. 
Also, the general trend is that the $\Delta x = 0.1$ grid generally has a higher error, as expected. For $\Delta x = 0.01$ the error is around $40\times$ smaller than than $\Delta x = 0.1$ on a one week contract, but around $100\times$ better for a one year contract. Close to maturity, a finer grid gives less gain due to the kink.

In Figure~\ref{fig:fd_convergence} we investigate how the 
scheme performs for different $\Delta x$, looking at solutions of the call option at $1$, $4$, $13$, and $52$ weeks to maturity. For $\Delta x = 0.01$ the plot clearly shows that the error is greatest near maturity, i.e., when we price an option with 1 week to maturity. As in Figure~\ref{fig:fd_profiles} the errors here also grows with a coarser grid. The error scales with a slope and $2$ (like $\sin(\pi x)$) for all curves on fine grids, and for 13- and 52-weeks on all grids. But, close to maturity, the slope is 2 only on fine grids. 

In contrast to the sin function, the error for the Option does not fall like $\Delta x^2$ on all grids. On rougher grids the scheme notice the kink more, as the grid is too rough to see a smooth curve, hence the error falls slower there. The error diminishes for all grids but for $\Delta x \geq\sigma \sqrt{T - t}$, the error diminishes slower than $\Delta x^2$. We see that after the blue dashed line, the 1 week error continues to grow for increased $\Delta x$ but in a diminishing manner. Same goes for the 4 week contract, which grows slower after the orange dashed line.

Finally, we turn to justifying why we use $x \in [-L, L] = [-1, 1]$. We ran the shcme for each combination of our right boundary, and $L = 0.5,\, 1.0,\, 1.5$ on $\Delta x = 0.01$ and see where the bigges error lies. For the left boundary we say that the option is worth $0$. At the right boundary we ran with  $C \approx S$ and $C = S - K e^{-r(T -t)}$, ie. the stock price minus the discounted present value you pay. The first method yields an error of $\sim 100$ from the true option price at the boundary. It forgets that we have to pay $K$ for the stock. The second method correctly places the biggest error near strike, and a small error of $0.017$ on one week and $0.002$ on a year, so the boundary does not contribute. Using $L = 0.5$ we get the largest error in the left boundary of $0.064$ ($S = 61)$ on a one year contract, but with one year left, the option is not worthless there. $L = 1.5$ yields the same result as for $L = 1$ ($0.017$ and $0.002$), so a wider domain does not improve the results. $L= 1$ leaves the rest of the error near the kink like we found.
